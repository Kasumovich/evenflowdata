AI-Capex Book-to-Bill Monitor

Orders are a leading indicator. The book-to-bill ratio leads revenue; the higher the ratio, the longer the revenue lead-time. A high ratio puts greater reliance on working capital to fund the activity that generates those future billings, making credit availability the key force behind cancellations and a market reassessment of future billings. Analyst Note
The September-quarter prints split the cohort by layer. The physical build re-accelerated: GE Vernova booked a record $29.3 billion of orders and lifted backlog by $20 billion to $197 billion, Parker's backlog hit a record $13.7 billion, Eaton's data-center orders are up more than 100% over twelve months, nVent printed record orders, and Vertiv says its orders more than doubled while still declining to give a dollar figure. Trane is the one physical name decelerating, with book-to-bill down to 107% from 123%, though its backlog still rose. The buyers kept signing: the five largest hyperscaler and cloud buyers now carry about $1.3 trillion of building leases whose rent has not started, up about $213 billion in a quarter, and Alphabet's backstops of partners' data-center payments rose to $48 billion from $17 billion at year-end. The strain sits in the middle, with the borrowers who turn those leases into capacity. In October, CoreWeave's bonds were reported trading near 15%, Nebius's chief executive said some funding sources had temporarily dried up, and the cancellation log took its first two AI-capex entries, both at Microsoft: it walked away as anchor tenant of an Nscale site in the UK whose grid power slipped to the 2030s, and it trimmed calendar-2026 capex to about $170 billion because third-party capacity arrived late. Both are supply slipping rather than demand pulling back, so the order book is still intact. The next ten days decide whether that holds, with IREN tonight, CoreWeave on November 10, Nebius and Forgent on November 12 and Powell on November 16.

The buyer side: what the hyperscalers have signed

The mirror image of the backlog above: one company's backlog is its customer's obligation. These are commitments signed but not yet delivered, taken from the quarterly filing footnotes. Leases not yet commenced are signed building leases whose rent has not started, so US accounting does not yet put them on the balance sheet. Purchase commitments are noncancelable contracts to buy chips, power, cloud capacity and equipment; they are obligations to pay, not borrowing. Guarantees are promises to cover a third party's loss or loan if it fails (a residual value guarantee covers a landlord's loss if a building is worth less than expected); they are contingent, so they are not added to the other two. $1,304B of leases not yet commenced across these five, +20% (+$213B) on the prior quarter. Direction is the signal: the quarterly addition shrinking would be the first sign the buyers are slowing.

What would flip this to CONTRACTION

  • Momentum turns lower across the hard names. Aggregate is 2.50x today, but 7 of 19 names already show order flow below their prior print. The signal that matters is FPS, POWL or AVAV printing book-to-bill lower than last quarter, not a soft EPS quarter and not any line at 1.0.
  • Buyer-side commitments stop growing. The hyperscalers' signed-but-undelivered leases and purchase commitments are the other side of the backlog above. If the quarterly addition shrinks while the sellers' backlog is still rising, or a guarantee is cut back, orders are running ahead of the people who must pay for them.
  • The cancellation log fills. Any AI-capex cancellation, push-out, or deferral moves the warning from the price into the orders. 2 logged so far.
  • Backlog growth stalls sequentially. Oracle's RPO already shows it (+$26B seq vs +$209B YoY). Watch whether FPS's seventh-straight-record streak breaks, and whether CIEN's next-month order pace fades.
  • Price leads and orders follow. FPS peaked in June with the order book still accelerating. If the next print finally shows book-to-bill rolling lower, the price was right and this monitor caught the hand-off from multiple to bookings.
