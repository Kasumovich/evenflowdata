Analyst Note
Book-to-bill's level still screams expansion, with the hard-reported aggregate near 2.5x and Powell booking three times its quarterly revenue, but the second derivative is where the cohort has started to split. Oracle's backlog is up 46% over the year yet added only $26 billion in the quarter, Argan's backlog is down $411 million since January as projects burn off faster than new awards arrive, and Vertiv stopped printing an orders or backlog figure in a quarter when its shares fell about 17% on a revenue miss, even as its customer prepayments reportedly doubled. The cleanest forward gauge now sits one layer down the chain: Micron holds $32 billion of customer commitments and $12.7 billion of deposits, so the flow of new deposits, not the balance, is what will say first when buyers start pricing to spot.

What would flip this to CONTRACTION

  • Book-to-bill crosses 1.0. Aggregate is 2.50x today. The first cohort name to print bookings below billings — especially FPS or a pure-play power name — is the signal that matters, not a soft EPS quarter.
  • The cancellation log fills. Any AI-capex cancellation, push-out, or deferral moves the warning from the price into the orders. Zero today.
  • Backlog growth stalls sequentially. Oracle's RPO already shows it (+$26B seq vs +$209B YoY). Watch whether FPS's seventh-straight-record streak breaks, and whether CIEN's next-month order pace fades.
  • Price leads and orders follow. FPS peaked in June with the order book still accelerating. If the next print finally shows book-to-bill rolling toward 1, the price was right and this monitor caught the hand-off from multiple to bookings.
