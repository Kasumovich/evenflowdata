AI-Capex Book-to-Bill Monitor

Orders are a leading indicator. The book-to-bill ratio leads revenue; the higher the ratio, the longer the revenue lead-time. A high ratio puts greater reliance on working capital to fund the activity that generates those future billings, making credit availability the key force behind cancellations and a market reassessment of future billings. Analyst Note
Book-to-bill's level still screams expansion, with the hard-reported aggregate near 2.5x and Powell booking three times its quarterly revenue, but the second derivative is where the cohort has started to split: ten names show order momentum higher than their prior print, seven lower, and two do not say. The lower group now reaches the borrowers: Oracle added $26 billion of backlog in the quarter against $85 billion the quarter before, CoreWeave about $5 billion against about $33 billion and Nebius a similar slowdown, while Argan's backlog is down $411 million since January and Vertiv has not printed an orders figure since its peak Q4 2025 report even as customer prepayments doubled. The cleanest forward gauge still sits one layer down the chain: Micron holds $32 billion of customer commitments and $12.7 billion of deposits, and new deposits are still arriving fast ($12.3 billion in the quarter against about $0.4 billion in the one before), but the flow, not the balance, is what will say first when buyers start pricing to spot.

What would flip this to CONTRACTION

  • Momentum turns lower across the hard names. Aggregate is 2.50x today, but 7 of 19 names already show order flow below their prior print. The signal that matters is FPS, POWL or AVAV printing book-to-bill lower than last quarter, not a soft EPS quarter and not any line at 1.0.
  • The cancellation log fills. Any AI-capex cancellation, push-out, or deferral moves the warning from the price into the orders. Zero today.
  • Backlog growth stalls sequentially. Oracle's RPO already shows it (+$26B seq vs +$209B YoY). Watch whether FPS's seventh-straight-record streak breaks, and whether CIEN's next-month order pace fades.
  • Price leads and orders follow. FPS peaked in June with the order book still accelerating. If the next print finally shows book-to-bill rolling lower, the price was right and this monitor caught the hand-off from multiple to bookings.
